# METEK editorial voice

Author: METEK Digital (founder: Nurullah Aydın)

Goal: Explain custom web and software work with the judgment of a working studio and the clarity of an experienced editor.

Confidence: medium-high; every factual claim still follows `proof-points.md`.

## The voice in one sentence

METEK sounds like the people who inspected the problem, made the work and can explain the decisions without decorating them.

## Point of view

- Default to **we / biz / wir / nosotros** for the agency. Use the founder's name and first-person singular only on the Agency/Manifesto surface.
- Write to a buyer who is comparing real options. Answer what was built, why that choice mattered and what the visitor can do next.
- Take an editorial position when the evidence supports one. A useful opinion is better than neutral filler.
- Do not describe METEK as a large team or a permanent solo practice. One accountable delivery lead is the supported claim.

## Information before atmosphere

- Give each paragraph one new piece of information. If removing a paragraph changes nothing, remove it.
- Prefer observable nouns and actions: quote form, catalogue filter, appointment slot, customer record, title tag, content panel.
- Adjectives cannot carry the claim. Words such as *professional*, *premium*, *strong*, *clear*, *calm*, *powerful*, *bespoke* and their locale equivalents need a visible reason or should be cut.
- Name the service where search intent needs it: custom website, admin panel, CRM, CSS design system, WhatsApp chatbot, Instagram chatbot, mobile app.
- SEO terms belong in titles, metadata and the natural first explanation. Never repeat them to fill a paragraph.

## Rhythm and structure

- Mix sentence lengths. Short sentences are useful, but three slogan fragments in a row are not.
- Vary paragraph length and openings. Do not make every project, card or article follow the same sentence template.
- Build articles in sequence: observation, consequence, decision, practical use. Paragraphs should not be freely interchangeable.
- A list is for genuinely parallel items such as checks or deliverables. Prose is the default for an argument.
- Use headings that state the subject. Avoid empty scaffolding such as “Overview,” “Why it matters,” “In conclusion” and their translations.
- Read every long-form draft aloud once. Rewrite any passage that sounds metronomic or rehearsed.

## Anti-AI rules

- No “not X, but Y” reveal, including the split form (“X is not the point. Y is.”). State the positive claim.
- No em-dash pivots in published prose. Use a full stop or rebuild the sentence.
- No rule-of-three padding, false ranges, rhetorical transition questions or manufactured punchlines.
- No generic openings about today's digital world. Start with the specific problem.
- No generic endings about the future, partnership or “taking the next step.” Finish with a decision, a useful check or a direct action.
- No synonym cycling. Repeat the precise noun when it remains the right noun.
- No copy that announces its own importance: “the key point,” “what is interesting,” “it is worth noting,” or equivalents.
- No agency filler: innovative solutions, seamless, cutting-edge, leverage, elevate, transform, future-proof, outcome-driven, design-led.
- No recurring closers such as “a short summary is enough,” “we will clarify it together,” “tek akış,” “net bir yol,” or “the site and panel speak the same language.” Say what information is needed or what the system shares.

## Surface rules

### Interface and navigation

- Use the fewest words that make the next action unambiguous.
- Buttons use an active verb and a concrete object. Avoid “Learn more,” “Submit,” and vague equivalents.
- Do not repeat the heading in the supporting sentence.

### Home and services

- Lead with the category of work and one proof-backed distinction.
- Service descriptions tell the buyer when that service fits. They do not praise the service.
- Keep one primary action. WhatsApp may remain a secondary route where already supported.

### Project cases

- Open on the business situation, not a string of design adjectives.
- Separate facts about the client from decisions made by METEK.
- Deliverables use specific objects and verbs. Avoid six bullets with identical grammar.
- Results describe the shipped surface and available user path. Do not imply revenue, traffic, ranking or conversion lifts without evidence.
- Calls to action ask for the few facts needed to scope a related project. Do not reuse one stock closer across the portfolio.

### Blog

- Write as an expert explaining a decision to a business owner, not as a keyword page pretending to be an essay.
- A post needs a defensible thesis, practical examples and a final recommendation. Each section must advance the argument.
- Explain technical terms on first use. Keep necessary terminology; remove ceremonial jargon.
- Use first-hand studio judgment where appropriate (“we check…”, “we prefer…”), without turning the article into a sales page.
- One restrained service reference near the end is enough.
- Review the series as a whole. Do not force the studio name or the same inquiry sentence into every closing paragraph; some articles should end on a test, decision or practical recommendation.

### Metadata

- Put the native search phrase near the front, then state the page's concrete value.
- Keep titles distinct by page. Descriptions are readable sentences, not comma-separated keyword inventories.

## Locale direction

- **tr-TR:** direct, literate Turkish. Avoid translated agency idioms and repeated words such as “net,” “doğru,” “güçlü,” “işleyen,” “akış” and “taşımak” as substitutes for detail.
- **en-US:** plain American professional English. Contractions are welcome where natural. Avoid British-only UI wording and pitch-deck cadence.
- **de-DE:** restrained, concrete Sie-form. Shorten compounds in headings and buttons; avoid unnecessary English agency terms.
- **es-US:** neutral US Hispanic business Spanish. Avoid Spain-only vocabulary, English calques and inflated corporate phrasing.

## Claim and quality rules

- Proof-tied claims only. No fabricated metrics, awards, testimonials, rankings, client counts or guaranteed outcomes.
- Portfolio markets and technologies may be named when present in the evidence files.
- UX-writing review comes before the final avoid-AI edit. The final edit must preserve facts, placeholders, markup and locale intent.
- A clean lint result is a floor. Publication also requires a human read for meaning, rhythm and mobile line breaks.
